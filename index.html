Rubber meets road/Lighting: darkness, crime and 311, multi-city

Where darkness, broken lights and crime overlap — five cities

Prototype maps adapting New York City's street-lighting tools to Chicago, Philadelphia, Baltimore and Los Angeles. New York is included too — its darkness layer rebuilt from the same satellite source, so all five are directly comparable. Each city has two companion maps built from open data on a shared hexagon grid.

These are drafts for review, not published work. Within each map the two data layers share the same time window; windows differ between cities because the available data does (Baltimore and Los Angeles crime feeds stop at the end of 2024 and 2023 respectively). The darkness layer everywhere is NASA's VIIRS Black Marble 2023 composite.

311 × crime

Crosses 311 streetlight-outage complaints with violent crime. Shows where residents report dark or broken lights and where crime is recorded — a ground-truth, resident-reported view.

Darkness × crime

Crosses calibrated nighttime satellite radiance — the Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) Black Marble composite — with violent crime, using percentile sliders to flag dark, high-crime cells. Mirrors the New York City satellite tool.

Method, in brief. Police-reported violent crime (assault, robbery and homicide, plus battery and shootings where a city codes them) is binned to a roughly block-scale hexagon grid. The 311 maps add streetlight-outage service requests; the satellite maps add 2023 VIIRS Black Marble radiance. Each map carries its own full methodology and caveats.

Reference: the original New York City darkness-and-crime map these adapt.
